classmethod doc_pde()[source]#

PDEs solved by model:

Vlasov equation:

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla f + \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \left( \mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}_0 \right) \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{v}} = 0\]

Ampère’s law:

\[-\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\varepsilon} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \mathbf{v} f \, \mathrm{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\]

Initial Poisson equation: At \(t=0\), solve weakly for the electric potential \(\phi\):

\[\begin{split}\int_{\Omega} \nabla \psi^{\top} \cdot \nabla \phi \, \mathrm{d} \mathbf{x} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\varepsilon} \int_{\Omega} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \psi \, (f - f_0) \, \mathrm{d}^3 \mathbf{v} \, \mathrm{d} \mathbf{x} \qquad \forall \ \psi \in H^1 \\[2mm] \mathbf{E}(t=0) = -\nabla \phi(t=0)\end{split}\]
classmethod doc_normalization()[source]#

Velocity and field normalizations:

\[\hat{v} = c, \qquad \hat{E} = \hat{B} \hat{v}, \qquad \hat{\phi} = \hat{E} \hat{x}\]

Dimensionless parameters:

\[\alpha = \frac{\hat{\omega}_\mathrm{p}}{\hat{\omega}_\mathrm{c}}, \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{1}{\hat{\omega}_\mathrm{c} \hat{t}}\]

where

\[\hat{\omega}_\mathrm{p} = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{n} (Ze)^2}{\epsilon_0 (A m_\mathrm{H})}}, \qquad \hat{\omega}_\mathrm{c} = \frac{(Ze) \hat{B}}{(A m_\mathrm{H})}\]

For electrons: \(Z = -1\), \(A = 1/1836\).

classmethod doc_scalar_quantities()[source]#

The following scalars are tracked during simulation:

  • Electric field energy en_E: \(\frac{1}{2} \int |\mathbf{E}|^2 \, \mathrm{d}V\)

  • Kinetic energy en_f: \(\frac{\alpha^2}{2N} \sum_p w_p |\mathbf{v}_p|^2\)

  • Total energy en_tot: en_E + en_f

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model couples the kinetic Vlasov equation for the particle distribution function with Ampère’s law for the electric field evolution. It includes the effect of a static background magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}_0\) and solves the initial Poisson equation to satisfy Gauss’s law at \(t=0\). The model uses a particle-in-cell (PIC) method with finite element exterior calculus (FEEC) for the electromagnetic fields.

All field variables are perturbations around a reference equilibrium distribution. The model enables studies of kinetic instabilities, particle-wave interactions, and nonlinear plasma physics without assuming a fluid approximation.

classmethod doc_pde()[source]#

PDEs solved by model:

Vlasov equation:

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla f + \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \left( \mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \left( \mathbf{B} + \mathbf{B}_0 \right) \right) \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{v}} = 0\]

Ampère’s law:

\[-\frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} + \nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\varepsilon} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \mathbf{v} f \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\]

Faraday’s law:

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} + \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = 0\]

where \(Z=-1\) and \(A=1/1836\) for electrons.

At initial time the weak Poisson equation is solved once to weakly satisfy Gauss’ law,

\[\begin{split}\int_{\Omega} \nabla \psi^{\top} \cdot \nabla \phi \, \textrm{d} \mathbf{x} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\varepsilon} \int_{\Omega} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \psi \, (f - f_0) \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v} \, \textrm{d} \mathbf{x} \qquad \forall \ \psi \in H^1 \\[2mm] \mathbf{E}(t=0) = -\nabla \phi(t=0)\end{split}\]

Moreover, it is assumed that

\[\nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0 = \frac{\alpha^2}{\varepsilon} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \mathbf{v} f_0 \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\]

where \(\mathbf{B}_0\) is the static equilibrium magnetic field.

class struphy.models.VlasovMaxwellQuasiNeutral(base_units: BaseUnits = BaseUnits(x=1.0, B=1.0, n=1.0, kBT=None), charge_number: int = 1, mass_number: float = 1.0, alpha: float | None = None, epsilon: float | None = None, lam: float | None = None, kappa: float | None = None, measure_gauss_law: bool = False)[source]#

Bases: Vlasov

Vlasov-Maxwell equations for one kinetic species in the cyclotron normalization, with an implicit, energy conserving field-particle coupling that also holds in the quasi-neutral limit (no displacement current).

Same equations as VlasovMaxwellOneSpecies, normalized with the cyclotron frequency (velocity scale "cyclotron"), so that the coefficients \(\lambda^2\) of the displacement current and \(\kappa^2\) of the plasma current appear in Ampère’s law. Ampère’s law, Faraday’s law and the electric force on the particles are solved together in one Crank-Nicolson step (VlasovMaxwellCoupling); lam=0.0 is the quasi-neutral limit. It inherits the particle propagators of Vlasov (with the Lorentz force of the total magnetic field \(\mathbf B_0 + \mathbf B\)).

Parameters:

  • base_units (BaseUnits) – Base units for normalization (default: BaseUnits())

  • charge_number (int) – Charge number (in units of the positive elementary charge) of the species (default: 1)

  • mass_number (float) – Mass number (in units of Proton mass) of the species (default: 1.0)

  • alpha (float, optional) – Dimensionless parameter: plasma frequency / cyclotron frequency. If None, computed from units and charge/mass numbers. It does not enter the equations of this model (which use lam and kappa = alpha lam); when overriding lam and kappa, alpha is not adapted.

  • epsilon (float, optional) – Normalized cyclotron period: 1 / (cyclotron frequency × time unit). If None, computed from units and charge/mass numbers (equal to 1 in this model’s normalization).

  • lam (float, optional) – Cyclotron frequency × length unit / speed of light (coefficient lam^2 of the displacement current; 0 is the quasi-neutral limit). If None, computed from units and charge/mass numbers.

  • kappa (float, optional) – Plasma frequency × length unit / speed of light (coefficient kappa^2 of the plasma current). If None, computed from units and charge/mass numbers.

  • measure_gauss_law (bool) – Whether to track the Gauss-law error as a scalar quantity (default: False; not available for lam = 0)

classmethod doc_pde()[source]#

PDEs solved by model:

Vlasov equation:

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla f + \left( \mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \left( \mathbf{B} + \mathbf{B}_0 \right) \right) \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{v}} = 0\]

Ampère’s law:

\[-\lambda^2 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t} + \nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \kappa^2 \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \mathbf{v} f \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\]

Faraday’s law:

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} + \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = 0\]

For \(\lambda = 0\) (quasi-neutral limit) Ampère’s law becomes \(\nabla \times \mathbf B = \kappa^2 \int \mathbf v f \, \textrm d^3 \mathbf v\), which implies \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf J = 0\); the electric field is then determined by this constraint, not by its own evolution. The coefficients of the coupling terms can be changed by the options lam, c1, c2, c3 of the propagator VlasovMaxwellCoupling.

At initial time the weak Poisson equation (Gauss’ law \(\lambda^2 \nabla \cdot \mathbf E = \kappa^2 \rho\)) is solved once for \(\lambda > 0\),

\[\begin{split}\lambda^2 \int_{\Omega} \nabla \psi^{\top} \cdot \nabla \phi \, \textrm{d} \mathbf{x} = \kappa^2 \int_{\Omega} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \psi \, (f - f_0) \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v} \, \textrm{d} \mathbf{x} \qquad \forall \ \psi \in H^1 \\[2mm] \mathbf{E}(t=0) = -\nabla \phi(t=0)\end{split}\]

(for \(\lambda = 0\) the initial electric field does not enter the time stepping). Moreover, it is assumed that \(\nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0 = \kappa^2 \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \mathbf{v} f_0 \, \text{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\), where \(\mathbf{B}_0\) is the static equilibrium magnetic field.

classmethod doc_normalization()[source]#

The model uses the cyclotron velocity of the species as reference velocity:

\[\hat v = \hat\Omega_c \hat x,\qquad \hat t = \frac{1}{\hat\Omega_c},\qquad \hat E = \hat B \hat v,\qquad \hat\phi = \hat E \hat x.\]

The species parameters (see Equation parameters) are

\[\lambda = \frac{\hat\Omega_c \hat x}{c}\,,\qquad \kappa = \frac{\hat\Omega_p \hat x}{c} = \alpha \lambda\,,\]

where \(c/\hat\Omega_c\) and \(c/\hat\Omega_p\) are the inertial lengths (\(\lambda\) carries the sign of the charge, like \(\hat\Omega_c\); only \(\lambda^2\) enters). The Vlasov equation and Faraday’s law then have no coefficients, and Ampère’s law \(\frac{1}{c^2} \partial_t \mathbf E = \nabla \times \mathbf B - \mu_0 \mathbf J\) becomes \(\lambda^2 \partial_t \mathbf E = \nabla \times \mathbf B - \kappa^2 \mathbf J\). The quasi-neutral limit is \(\lambda \to 0\) at fixed \(\kappa\). With \(\lambda = \kappa = 1\) the equations are those of VlasovMaxwellOneSpecies with \(\alpha = 1\), i.e. lengths in units of \(c/\hat\Omega_p\) and \(\hat\Omega_c = \hat\Omega_p\).

classmethod doc_scalar_quantities()[source]#

The following scalars are tracked during simulation (the parts of the energy conserved by the coupling step, with the coefficients \(\lambda^2, c_1, c_2, c_3\) of VlasovMaxwellCoupling):

  • Electric field energy \(\frac{\lambda^2}{2} \int |\mathbf E|^2 \textrm d \mathbf x\): en_E

  • Magnetic field energy \(\frac{c_1}{2} \int |\mathbf B|^2 \textrm d \mathbf x\): en_B

  • Particle kinetic energy \(\frac{c_2}{2 c_3} \sum_p w_p |\mathbf v_p|^2\): en_f (\(\frac{\kappa^2}{2} \sum_p w_p |\mathbf v_p|^2\) for the default coefficients)

  • Total energy: en_tot (conserved by the coupling step up to the solver tolerance)

  • Optional Gauss-law diagnostic: gauss_error (NaN for \(\lambda = 0\))

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

VlasovMaxwellQuasiNeutral solves the Vlasov-Maxwell system in the cyclotron normalization, with one implicit step for Maxwell’s equations and the electric force, which conserves the energy exactly and allows time steps larger than the inverse plasma frequency and the light-wave CFL limit. The coefficient \(\lambda^2\) of the displacement current can be set to zero (lam=0), which gives the quasi-neutral (Darwin-type) limit, in which the electric field is the Lagrange multiplier of a divergence-free current.

class struphy.models.VlasovPoissonAdiabatic(base_units: BaseUnits = BaseUnits(x=1.0, B=1.0, n=1.0, kBT=1.0), charge_number: int = 1, mass_number: float = 1.0, alpha: float | None = None, epsilon: float | None = None)[source]#

Bases: Vlasov

Electrostatic full-orbit (6D) model for a single ion species with adiabatic electrons.

The full-orbit counterpart of DriftKineticElectrostaticAdiabatic: the ions are not reduced to guiding centers, so the polarization density is not modelled but comes out of the ion orbits. The electrons enter through the adiabatic (Boltzmann) response in the Poisson equation, optionally with the same flux-surface average as in the drift-kinetic model. Extends Vlasov by the electrostatic force and the Poisson equation.

Parameters:

  • base_units (BaseUnits) – Base units for normalization (default: BaseUnits(kBT=1.0)).

  • charge_number (int) – Charge number (in units of the positive elementary charge) of the ion species (default: 1).

  • mass_number (float) – Mass number (in units of the proton mass) of the ion species (default: 1.0).

  • alpha (float, optional) – Ratio of plasma to cyclotron frequency \(\hat \omega_p / \hat \Omega_c\). If None, computed from base_units and the charge/mass numbers.

  • epsilon (float, optional) – Normalized inverse cyclotron frequency \(1 / (\hat{\Omega}_\mathrm{c} \hat{t})\). If None, computed from base_units and the charge/mass numbers.

classmethod doc_pde()[source]#

PDEs solved by model:

Vlasov equation:

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla f + \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \left( -\nabla \phi + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}_0 \right) \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{v}} = 0\]

Poisson equation with adiabatic electrons:

\[-\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha^2} \Delta \phi + \frac{1}{Z \varepsilon} \frac{n_0}{T_0} \left( \phi - \langle \phi \rangle \right) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f \, \mathrm{d}^3 \mathbf{v} - n_0\]

where \(f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}, t)\) is the ion distribution function, \(n_0\) the equilibrium ion density, \(T_0\) the electron temperature and \(Z\) the ion charge number. The vacuum term \(-\varepsilon \alpha^{-2} \Delta \phi\) is dropped in the quasi-neutral limit \(\alpha \to \infty\) (option quasi_neutral). The flux-surface average \(\langle \phi \rangle\) (zonal correction, as in DriftKineticElectrostaticAdiabatic) is optional; by default \(\langle \phi \rangle = 0\).

classmethod doc_normalization()[source]#

The reference speed is the ion thermal speed and the electrostatic fields are scaled accordingly:

\[\hat v = \sqrt{k_B \hat T / m_i},\qquad \hat E = \hat v \hat B,\qquad \hat\phi = \hat E \hat x,\]

with temperatures in units of \(k_B \hat T\). The dimensionless parameters are

\[\varepsilon = \frac{1}{\hat\Omega_c\hat t} = \frac{\hat \rho}{\hat x}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\hat{\omega}_\mathrm{p}}{\hat{\Omega}_\mathrm{c}},\]

such that \(\varepsilon / \alpha\) is the normalized Debye length.

classmethod doc_scalar_quantities()[source]#

The following scalars are tracked during simulation (their sum is conserved by the model equations):

  • Field energy: en_phi \(= \frac{1}{2\alpha^2} \int |\nabla \phi|^2 \, \mathrm d \mathbf x + \frac{1}{2 Z \varepsilon^2} \int \frac{n_0}{T_0} \phi (\phi - \langle \phi \rangle) \, \mathrm d \mathbf x\) (the first term only if quasi_neutral=False)

  • Kinetic energy: en_f \(= \frac{1}{2} \sum_p w_p |\mathbf{v}_p|^2\) (of \(f - f_0\) with the control variate)

  • Total energy: en_tot

classmethod doc_discretization()[source]#

Time integration is performed by the following propagators (in sequence). The Poisson solve comes right after the drift, such that with Strang splitting both velocity kicks use the potential of the current marker positions (drift-kick-kick-drift). Since a Strang step ends with the half drift, the saved potential and en_phi belong to the marker positions half a step earlier.

  1. push_eta: PushEta

  2. poisson: AdiabaticPoissonSolve

  3. push_v_E: PushVinForceField

  4. push_vxb: PushVxB

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model is the full-orbit (6D) counterpart of the electrostatic drift-kinetic model DriftKineticElectrostaticAdiabatic. The ions follow full Lorentz-force orbits in the self-consistent electrostatic field and a static background magnetic field. Electrons are not evolved; they enter through a linearized Boltzmann response in the Poisson equation, optionally without response to the flux-surface-averaged potential (zonal correction), as in the drift-kinetic model. The potential is recomputed from the marker positions at each time step.

In the strong-field limit \(\varepsilon \to 0\) with drift ordering, the linear dispersion relation of this model reduces to that of the drift-kinetic model (up to finite-Larmor-radius corrections of order \(k_\perp^2 \rho_i^2\)), which makes it a reference for verifying guiding-center models. The time step must resolve the cyclotron motion, \(\Delta t \lesssim \varepsilon\). In the quasi-neutral limit it must also resolve the ion-acoustic frequency of the smallest cells, \(\Delta t\, c_s \pi / h \lesssim 2\) (there is no Debye cutoff).

PDEs solved by model:

Vlasov equation for classical Pauli particles, with the magnetic moment \(\mu \geq 0\) as a parameter of the distribution function \(f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}, \mu, t)\):

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla f + \left[ \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \left( \mathbf{E}^\dagger + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B}_0 \right) \right] \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf{v}} = 0\,, \qquad \mathbf{E}^\dagger = -\nabla \left( \phi + \varepsilon \mu |\mathbf{B}_0| \right)\,,\]

with \(f\) concentrated on the slow manifold \(\mathbf v \times \mathbf b_0 \approx 0\). Poisson equation with adiabatic electrons:

\[-\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha^2} \Delta \phi + \frac{1}{Z \varepsilon} \frac{n_0}{T_0} \left( \phi - \langle \phi \rangle \right) = \int_0^\infty \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} f \, \mathrm{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\, \mathrm{d} \mu - n_0\,,\]

where \(n_0\) is the equilibrium ion density, \(T_0\) the electron temperature and \(Z\) the ion charge number. On the slow manifold the marker positions are guiding-center positions, so the charge density is the guiding-center density (no gyro-average). With full-f weights the polarization density comes out of the marker orbits (compression by the polarization drift). With the control variate it is removed from the deposited density (see the long description) and is added in the quasi-neutrality equation, as for guiding centers (option polarization of the Poisson solve):

\[-\varepsilon \nabla \cdot \left( \frac{n_0}{|\mathbf B_0|^2} \nabla_\perp \phi \right) + \frac{1}{Z \varepsilon} \frac{n_0}{T_0} \left( \phi - \langle \phi \rangle \right) = \int_0^\infty \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} (f - f_0) \, \mathrm{d}^3 \mathbf{v}\, \mathrm{d} \mu\,.\]

As in VlasovPoissonAdiabatic, the vacuum term is dropped in the quasi-neutral limit (option quasi_neutral) and the flux-surface average \(\langle \phi \rangle\) is optional.

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

Guiding-center dynamics computed with the slow manifold of the classical Pauli particle (J. Xiao and H. Qin, “Slow manifolds of classical Pauli particle enable structure-preserving geometric algorithms for guiding center dynamics”, Comput. Phys. Commun. 265, 107981 (2021)). A classical Pauli particle is a charged particle with an intrinsic magnetic moment \(\mu\), with the Lagrangian \(L = \frac{1}{2}|\dot{\mathbf x}|^2 + \dot{\mathbf x} \cdot \mathbf A - \phi - \mu B\). When its perpendicular velocity vanishes initially, its effective magnetic moment \(\mu' = |\dot{\mathbf x} \times \mathbf b|^2 / (2B)\) is an adiabatic invariant and stays close to zero: the particle moves on a slow manifold, along which its position follows the guiding-center dynamics of a particle with magnetic moment \(\mu\). Unlike the guiding-center Lagrangian, the Lagrangian of the classical Pauli particle is regular, so the full-orbit Boris scheme can be used with time steps larger than the cyclotron period.

The model is the counterpart of VlasovPoissonAdiabatic (full orbits, with finite-Larmor-radius effects) and DriftKineticElectrostaticAdiabatic (guiding centers with the polarization density in the Poisson equation): the ions have no gyration, hence no finite-Larmor-radius effects, and the polarization drift comes out of the orbits. The magnetic moment of each marker is taken from its drawn perpendicular velocity (PauliParticles6D), such that the markers represent the gyro-average of the initial condition, given in the guiding-center position.

Polarization and delta-f. The polarization drift on the slow manifold is compressible, so the phase-space density of the markers is not constant along their orbits. With full-f weights this compression gives the polarization density. The control variate subtracts the background \(f_0\) along the markers, which removes the compression part \(n_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol \xi\) of the polarization density (\(\boldsymbol \xi\) the polarization displacement) from the deposited charge; the advection part \(\boldsymbol \xi \cdot \nabla n_0\) remains in the weights. Without correction, a slab drift wave then has the frequency without polarization and becomes unstable at grid scale. With the guiding-center polarization term in the Poisson equation (polarization=True, the symmetric operator \(-\nabla \cdot (n_0 |\mathbf B_0|^{-2} \nabla_\perp \phi)\) of the drift-kinetic model, which also contains the advection part), the delta-f slab drift wave has the drift-kinetic frequency, also with time steps of 8 and 16 cyclotron periods. A weak instability remains, which needs the density gradient and is independent of the time step: in the slab example \(\mu'\) stays below \(5 \cdot 10^{-3}\) up to t = 25 and grows by a factor of about 5 per 5 time units and the field energy grows from t = 25 on (about one wave period). A likely cause is that the compression part of the polarization is now instantaneous (field equation), while its advection part follows the marker orbits, including their cyclotron response. Removing only the advection part from the polarization term does not change it. Full-f simulations of small perturbations are dominated by the noise, since the perpendicular ion response is that of cold ions.

PDEs solved by model:

Drift-kinetic equation for \(f(\mathbf X, v_\parallel, \mu, t)\):

\[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \frac{v_\parallel \mathbf B^* + \mathbf E^* \times \mathbf b_0}{B^*_\parallel} \cdot \nabla f + \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \frac{\mathbf B^* \cdot \mathbf E^*}{B^*_\parallel} \frac{\partial f}{\partial v_\parallel} = 0\,,\]

\[\mathbf E^* = \mathbf E - \varepsilon \mu \nabla |\mathbf B|\,,\qquad \mathbf B^* = \mathbf B_0 + \tilde{\mathbf B} + \varepsilon v_\parallel \nabla \times \mathbf b_0\,,\qquad B^*_\parallel = \mathbf b_0 \cdot \mathbf B^*\,,\]

where \(|\mathbf B| = \mathbf b_0 \cdot (\mathbf B_0 + \tilde{\mathbf B})\): the total magnetic field in the first term of \(\mathbf B^*\) (parallel streaming along the perturbed field lines), the curvature term with the equilibrium unit vector \(\mathbf b_0\).

Ampère’s law, with the displacement current and the (linearized) polarization current of the guiding centers:

\[\varepsilon^2 \lambda^2 \frac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t} + \varepsilon^2 \kappa^2 \frac{n_0}{|\mathbf B_0|^2} \frac{\partial \mathbf E_\perp}{\partial t} = \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf B} - \varepsilon \kappa^2 \iint f B^*_\parallel \left( v_\parallel \frac{\mathbf B^*}{B^*_\parallel} + \frac{\mathbf E \times \mathbf b_0}{B^*_\parallel} \right) \textrm d v_\parallel \textrm d \mu\,,\]

Faraday’s law:

\[\frac{\partial \tilde{\mathbf B}}{\partial t} + \nabla \times \mathbf E = 0\,.\]

\(\mathbf B_0\) is a static equilibrium field, \(n_0\) the equilibrium density, and \(\mathbf E_\perp = \mathbf E - (\mathbf b_0 \cdot \mathbf E) \mathbf b_0\). The current in Ampère’s law is the guiding-center current without the magnetic (grad-\(B\)) drift, whose work on \(\mathbf E\) is therefore missing where \(\nabla |B| \neq 0\). The coefficients \(\varepsilon\lambda\) (option lam, 0 is the quasi-neutral limit), \((\varepsilon\kappa)^2\) (option chi) and \(\varepsilon\kappa^2\) (option c2) can be changed in DriftKineticMaxwellCoupling. Without the polarization current the quasi-neutral limit would be singular for flute modes (\(k_\parallel = 0\)).

The model uses the thermal velocity of the species as reference velocity:

\[\hat v = \hat v_\textrm{th} = \sqrt{\frac{k_\textrm{B} \hat T}{m}}\,,\qquad \hat t = \frac{\hat x}{\hat v}\,, \qquad \hat E = \hat v \hat B\,,\qquad \hat \mu = \frac{m \hat v^2}{\hat B}\,.\]

The drift-kinetic equation and Faraday’s law then have only the coefficient \(\varepsilon\) (\(\frac{m \hat v}{q \hat B \hat x} = \varepsilon\) in \(\mathbf B^*\) and \(\mathbf E^*\), \(\frac{q \hat B \hat t}{m} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\) in the parallel force). \[\frac{\hat v^2}{c^2} = \varepsilon^2 \lambda^2\,,\qquad \frac{\mu_0 \hat n m \hat v^2}{\hat B^2} = \frac{\hat v^2}{\hat v_A^2} = \varepsilon^2 \kappa^2\,,\qquad \frac{\mu_0 q \hat n \hat v \hat x}{\hat B} = \varepsilon \kappa^2\,.\]

Their ratio \(\kappa^2/\lambda^2 = \hat\Omega_p^2/\hat\Omega_c^2 = c^2/\hat v_A^2\) compares the polarization with the vacuum dielectric, and \(1/\kappa\) is the inertial length \(c/\hat\Omega_p\) in units of \(\hat x\). classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

DriftKineticMaxwell couples one drift-kinetic (guiding-center) species to Maxwell’s equations in the gauge-invariant \(v_\parallel\) formulation (the electric field, not a potential, enters \(\mathbf E^*\)), normalized with the thermal velocity of the species. Maxwell’s equations and the parallel acceleration are solved together in one implicit, energy conserving step, which allows time steps above the plasma period and has no cancellation problem. Since the drift-kinetic ordering removes the polarization drift, its (linearized) polarization current is added to Ampère’s law; with it, the quasi-neutral limit without displacement current is well-posed and gives the inertial Alfvén wave. The guiding centers then stream along the field and drift with \(\mathbf E^* \times \mathbf b_0\). The total energy is conserved exactly by the splitting if \(\nabla |B_0| = 0\) and either \(\mu = 0\) or \(\mathbf b_0 \cdot \tilde{\mathbf B} = 0\).

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model simulates small-amplitude perturbations of a compressible, inviscid fluid around a static equilibrium. It solves the linearized Euler equations for the density, velocity and pressure perturbations. Its only waves are sound waves; with a non-uniform equilibrium density they are refracted.

This is the parent model of the LinearEuler family: LinearMHD adds the magnetic field (shear-Alfvén waves and the background current), and inherits the species, propagators and scalars of this model.

The time step is split into the sound wave step, which conserves the perturbed energy \(\frac 12 \int \rho_0 |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x + \frac{1}{2\gamma} \int \tilde p^2 / p_0 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x\) exactly (Crank-Nicolson with the \(1/p_0\)-weighted pressure equation), and the exact-in-time step of the term \(- \tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \nabla p_0\), which vanishes for an equilibrium in force balance. The two steps do not commute; they are integrated with symmetric (Strang) splitting, the sound wave step in the middle, such that the time step is second order in time (also with Time(split_algo="LieTrotter")).

The equilibrium is given by an MHD or fluid equilibrium (equil) providing \(n_0\) and \(p_0\); a magnetic field of the equilibrium is not used. With free boundary conditions the pressure term imposes \(\tilde p = 0\) weakly at the boundary; with essential boundary conditions on the velocity (Dirichlet in the de Rham sequence), \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) holds strongly (rigid wall).

\[\rho_0 \frac{\partial \tilde{\mathbf{U}}}{\partial t} + \nabla \tilde{p} = (\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}) \times \mathbf{B}_0 + (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}\]

Energy (adiabatic process):

\[\frac{\partial \tilde{p}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (p_0 \tilde{\mathbf{U}}) + \frac{2}{3} p_0 \nabla \cdot \tilde{\mathbf{U}} = 0\]

Induction (Faraday’s law):

\[\frac{\partial \tilde{\mathbf{B}}}{\partial t} - \nabla \times (\tilde{\mathbf{U}} \times \mathbf{B}_0) = 0.\]

Here, \(\tilde \rho\), \(\tilde{\mathbf U}\), \(\tilde p\), \(\tilde{\mathbf B}\) are the perturbed density, velocity, pressure and magnetic field, and \(\rho_0\), \(p_0\), \(\mathbf B_0\) those of a static MHD equilibrium; \(\gamma = 5/3\).

Conserved quantities (for \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) and \(\mathbf B_0 \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) on the boundary, or periodic boundaries):

Mass perturbation and divergence of the magnetic field:

\[\frac{\textnormal d}{\textnormal d t} \int \tilde \rho \, \textnormal d \mathbf x = 0\,, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \nabla \cdot \tilde{\mathbf B} = 0\]

Perturbed energy, for an equilibrium with \(\nabla \times \mathbf B_0 = 0\) and \(\nabla p_0 = 0\):

\[\frac{\textnormal d}{\textnormal d t} \int \left( \frac{\rho_0}{2} |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 + \frac 12 |\tilde{\mathbf B}|^2 + \frac{\tilde p^2}{2 \gamma p_0} \right) \textnormal d \mathbf x = 0\]

For general equilibria and perturbations generated by a plasma displacement \(\boldsymbol \xi\) (\(\partial_t \boldsymbol \xi = \tilde{\mathbf U}\), \(\tilde{\mathbf B} = \nabla \times (\boldsymbol \xi \times \mathbf B_0)\), \(\tilde p = - \boldsymbol \xi \cdot \nabla p_0 - \gamma p_0 \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol \xi\), e.g. for a pure velocity perturbation at \(t = 0\)), the conserved energy is the sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy \(\delta W(\boldsymbol \xi)\) (energy principle of ideal MHD).

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model simulates small-amplitude perturbations in a magnetized plasma with a static equilibrium magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}_0\) and zero background flow. The model solves the linearized ideal magnetohydrodynamic equations, which couple fluid dynamics (density, velocity, pressure) with magnetic field evolution.

The linear MHD system supports three wave families:

  • Alfvén waves: incompressible shear waves propagating along \(\mathbf{B}_0\)

  • Fast magnetosonic waves: compressible waves with phase velocity above the Alfvén speed

  • Slow magnetosonic waves: compressible waves with phase velocity below the Alfvén speed

All evolved quantities are perturbations around a stationary equilibrium:

  • \(\tilde{\rho}\) - density perturbation

  • \(\tilde{\mathbf{U}}\) - velocity perturbation

  • \(\tilde{p}\) - pressure perturbation

  • \(\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\) - magnetic field perturbation

The corresponding equilibrium quantities are:

  • \(\rho_0\) - background density

  • \(p_0\) - background pressure

  • \(\mathbf{B}_0\) - background magnetic field

Two discretizations of the magnetic field are available through the flag b_space. With "Hdiv" (default), \(\tilde{\mathbf B}\) is a 2-form and the induction equation holds strongly. With "Hcurl", \(\tilde{\mathbf B}\) is a 1-form, the induction equation is weak and the implicit shear-Alfvén step solves a field-aligned curl-curl system for the magnetic field, preconditioned by Hiptmair-Xu, which allows large time steps; the force \((\nabla \times \mathbf B_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf B}\) of the background current step then enters through the mixed mass matrix M21J. This shear-Alfvén step damps the waves numerically, with a damping that vanishes quickly with the resolution along the background field; projected=True gives the exactly energy conserving version, at the price of an inner mass matrix solve in every application of the Schur operator.

Boundary conditions with b_space="Hcurl": essential boundary conditions in the de Rham sequence set the tangential components of the magnetic field to zero on that boundary, which is unphysical at a wall with \(\mathbf B_0 \cdot \mathbf n = 0\). Free boundary conditions are therefore recommended: they impose \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) weakly in the shear-Alfvén step and \(\tilde p = 0\) weakly in the sound wave step (natural boundary condition of the pressure term).

The species, the sound wave step and the background pressure step are inherited from LinearEuler. The compressional part of the time step thus consists of three steps: the sound wave step, which conserves the energy \(\frac 12 \int \rho_0 |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x + \frac{1}{2\gamma} \int \tilde p^2 / p_0 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x\) exactly (Crank-Nicolson with the \(1/p_0\)-weighted pressure equation), and the steps of the two terms that change the perturbed energy for general equilibria, \((\nabla \times \mathbf B_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf B}\) in the momentum equation and \(- \tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \nabla p_0\) in the pressure equation, which are solved exactly in time. The three steps do not commute; they form one block of the splitting with symmetric (Strang) splitting, the sound wave step in the middle, so that the compressional part is second order in time as the former unsplit magnetosonic step (with Lie-Trotter sequencing of the three steps the frequencies of modes coupled to the compressional dynamics, e.g. TAEs in a torus, are wrong for large time steps). For an equilibrium with \(\nabla p_0 = 0\) and \(\nabla \times \mathbf B_0 = 0\), the scheme is thus energy conserving up to the solver tolerances (with b_space="Hdiv" or projected=True).

Momentum:

\[\rho_0 \frac{\partial \tilde{\mathbf{U}}}{\partial t} + \nabla (2 q_0 \tilde q) = 0\]

Square root of the pressure (adiabatic process):

\[\frac{\partial \tilde{q}}{\partial t} + \tilde{\mathbf{U}} \cdot \nabla q_0 + \frac{\gamma}{2} q_0 \nabla \cdot \tilde{\mathbf{U}} = 0\,.\]

Here, \(\tilde \rho\), \(\tilde{\mathbf U}\), \(\tilde q\) are the perturbed density, velocity and square root of the pressure, \(\rho_0\), \(q_0 = \sqrt{p_0}\) those of a static equilibrium (zero flow, \(\mathbf U_0 = 0\)), and \(\gamma = 5/3\). The equation of \(q\) is the linearization of \(\partial_t q + \mathbf U \cdot \nabla q + \frac{\gamma}{2} q \nabla \cdot \mathbf U = 0\), which follows from the adiabatic pressure equation with \(p = q^2\); the pressure perturbation is \(\tilde p = 2 q_0 \tilde q\), and the equations are those of LinearEuler written in \(\tilde q\). Force balance of the equilibrium requires \(\nabla q_0 = 0\); the term \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \nabla q_0\) is nevertheless kept, since it is part of the models of the family whose equilibria carry a pressure gradient (e.g. LinearMHDWithQ). For constant \(\rho_0\), \(q_0\) the perturbations are sound waves with the speed \(c_\textnormal{s} = \sqrt{\gamma q_0^2 / \rho_0}\).

Conserved quantities (for \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) on the boundary, or periodic boundaries):

Mass perturbation:

\[\frac{\textnormal d}{\textnormal d t} \int \tilde \rho \, \textnormal d \mathbf x = 0\]

Perturbed energy, for an equilibrium with \(\nabla q_0 = 0\):

\[\frac{\textnormal d}{\textnormal d t} \int \left( \frac{\rho_0}{2} |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 + \frac{2}{\gamma} \tilde q^2 \right) \textnormal d \mathbf x = 0\]

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model simulates small-amplitude perturbations of a compressible, inviscid fluid around a static equilibrium. It solves the linearized Euler equations for the perturbations of the density, the velocity and the square root of the pressure, \(q = \sqrt p\). Its only waves are sound waves; with a non-uniform equilibrium density they are refracted. It solves the same equations as LinearEuler, written in \(\tilde q = \tilde p / (2 q_0)\); it is the parent of the family with the variable \(q\), whose nonlinear (perturbative) models keep the terms quadratic in \(\tilde q\) in the energy.

LinearMHDWithQ adds the magnetic field (shear-Alfvén waves and the background current), and inherits the species, propagators and scalars of this model.

The time step is split into the sound wave step, which conserves the perturbed energy \(\frac 12 \int \rho_0 |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x + \frac{2}{\gamma} \int \tilde q^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x\) exactly (Crank-Nicolson; the force is the exact transpose of the operator of the \(\tilde q\) equation, so the discrete spectrum is purely imaginary and the step does not damp or amplify the waves), and the exact-in-time step of the term \(- \tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \nabla q_0\), which vanishes for an equilibrium in force balance. The two steps do not commute; they are integrated with symmetric (Strang) splitting, the sound wave step in the middle, such that the time step is second order in time (also with Time(split_algo="LieTrotter")).

The equilibrium is given by an MHD or fluid equilibrium (equil) providing \(n_0\) and \(p_0\); a magnetic field of the equilibrium is not used. With free boundary conditions the pressure term imposes \(\tilde q = 0\) weakly at the boundary; with essential boundary conditions on the velocity (Dirichlet in the de Rham sequence), \(\tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) holds strongly (rigid wall).

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

This model simulates small-amplitude perturbations in a magnetized plasma with a static equilibrium magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}_0\) and zero background flow. It solves the linearized ideal MHD equations of LinearMHD with the square root of the pressure, \(q = \sqrt p\), instead of the pressure; it is the linear limit of the perturbative MHD model in \(q\), and supports the same waves (shear Alfvén, fast and slow magnetosonic).

The magnetic field and its two discretizations (flag b_space: "Hdiv" with the strong induction equation, "Hcurl" with the weak induction equation and the field-aligned curl-curl solve, exactly energy conserving with projected=True) are those of LinearMHD, shared through MagneticFieldMixin. The species, the sound wave step and the background \(q\) step are inherited from LinearEulerWithQ.

The compressional part of the time step consists of three steps: the sound wave step, which conserves the energy \(\frac 12 \int \rho_0 |\tilde{\mathbf U}|^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x + \frac{2}{\gamma} \int \tilde q^2 \,\textnormal d \mathbf x\) exactly (Crank-Nicolson), and the steps of the two terms that change the perturbed energy for general equilibria, \((\nabla \times \mathbf B_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf B}\) in the momentum equation and \(- \tilde{\mathbf U} \cdot \nabla q_0\) in the equation of \(\tilde q\), which are solved exactly in time. The three steps form one block of the splitting with symmetric (Strang) splitting, the sound wave step in the middle, so that the compressional part is second order in time. For an equilibrium with \(\nabla p_0 = 0\) and \(\nabla \times \mathbf B_0 = 0\), the scheme is thus energy conserving up to the solver tolerances (with b_space="Hdiv" or projected=True).

Boundary conditions with b_space="Hcurl": free boundary conditions are recommended, as for LinearMHD.

classmethod doc_long_description()[source]#

ShearAlfven is the incompressible magnetic-tension subsystem extracted from linear MHD. It is useful when one wants to isolate Alfvénic propagation from the full linear MHD model. Two discretizations are available through the flag b_space. With "Hdiv" (default), the magnetic field is a 2-form, the induction equation holds strongly and the implicit step solves for the velocity. With "Hcurl", the magnetic field is a 1-form, the induction equation is weak and the implicit step solves a field-aligned curl-curl system for the magnetic field, preconditioned by the Hiptmair-Xu auxiliary space preconditioner, which allows large time steps. This step damps the waves numerically, with a damping that vanishes quickly with the resolution along the background field; projected=True gives the exactly energy conserving version, at the price of an inner mass matrix solve in every application of the Schur operator (and no effective curl-curl preconditioner). With b_space="Hcurl", essential boundary conditions in the de Rham sequence set the tangential components of the magnetic field to zero on that boundary (stronger than \(\mathbf B \cdot \mathbf n = 0\)), so free boundary conditions are recommended for bounded domains.

PDEs solved by model:

The unknowns are the perturbations \((\tilde\rho, \tilde p, \tilde{\mathbf B})\) with respect to a zero-flow MHD equilibrium \((\rho_0, p_0, \mathbf B_0)\), and the velocity \(\mathbf u\).

Continuity:

\[\partial_t \tilde{\rho} + \nabla \cdot \left( (\tilde{\rho} + \rho_0) \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) = 0\]

Momentum:

\[\partial_t \left( (\tilde{\rho} + \rho_0) \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) + \nabla \cdot \left( (\tilde{\rho} + \rho_0) \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \otimes \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) + \nabla \tilde{p} + \mathbf{B}_0 \times \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \times \nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0 + \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \times \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} - \nabla \cdot \left( (\mu + \mu_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) = 0\]

Pressure:

\[\partial_t \tilde{p} + \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \cdot \nabla (\tilde{p} + p_0) + \gamma (\tilde{p} + p_0) \nabla \cdot \tilde{\mathbf{u}} = (\gamma - 1) \left( (\mu + \mu_a(\mathbf{x})) |\nabla \tilde{\mathbf{u}}|^2 + (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) |\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}|^2 \right)\]

Induction:

\[\partial_t \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \nabla \times \left( (\tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \mathbf{B}_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) + \nabla \times (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} = 0\]

Here \(\mu_a(\mathbf{x})\) and \(\eta_a(\mathbf{x})\) are artificial viscosity and resistivity coefficients.

Linear model (linear=True): the terms that are nonlinear in the perturbations are dropped,

\[\begin{split}&\partial_t \tilde{\rho} + \nabla \cdot (\rho_0 \tilde{\mathbf{u}}) = 0\,, \\[2mm] &\partial_t (\rho_0 \tilde{\mathbf{u}}) + \nabla \tilde{p} + \mathbf{B}_0 \times \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \times \nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0 - \nabla \cdot \left( (\mu + \mu_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \right) = 0\,, \\[2mm] &\partial_t \tilde{p} + \tilde{\mathbf{u}} \cdot \nabla p_0 + \gamma p_0 \nabla \cdot \tilde{\mathbf{u}} = 0\,, \\[2mm] &\partial_t \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \nabla \times (\mathbf{B}_0 \times \tilde{\mathbf{u}}) + \nabla \times (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} = 0\,.\end{split}\]

In the linear model the viscous and resistive heating, which is quadratic in the perturbations, is not fed back into the pressure perturbation \(\tilde p\): it goes into the second-order auxiliary pressure \(\hat p\) (diagnostic pt3), whose energy \(\int \hat p/(\gamma - 1)\) is part of the conserved total energy en_tot. So with dissipation the total energy is still conserved up to the solver tolerances.

Velocity in \(H(\textnormal{div})\) (linear=True, crank_nicolson=True, u_space="Hdiv"): the linear model can also be discretized with the velocity as a 2-form, as in LinearMHD; this is possible because the linear model has no advection of the momentum (no commutator), the only term that needs \(\mathbf u \in (H^1)^3\). The variational structure is the same, so the energy is conserved up to the solver tolerances; the boundary condition is \(\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf n = 0\) instead of \(\mathbf u = 0\). It is available with one linear solve per substep only (crank_nicolson=True). The viscous term is then discretized with the divergence and the curl of \(\mathbf u\), \(\int \mu (\nabla \cdot \mathbf u\, \nabla \cdot \mathbf v + \nabla \times \mathbf u \cdot \nabla \times \mathbf v)\), which equals \(\int \mu \nabla \mathbf u : \nabla \mathbf v\) up to boundary terms. With Dirichlet boundary conditions the tangential trace of the curl argument is zero, which acts as a weak no-slip penalty (of order \(\mu/h\)) on the tangential velocity at the wall.

PDEs solved by model:

The unknowns are the perturbations \((\tilde\rho, \tilde q, \tilde{\mathbf B})\) with respect to a zero-flow MHD equilibrium \((\rho_0, q_0, \mathbf B_0)\) with \(q_0 = \sqrt{p_0}\), and the velocity \(\mathbf u\). The total quantities are \(\rho = \rho_0 + \tilde\rho\), \(q = q_0 + \tilde q = \sqrt{p}\) and \(\mathbf B = \mathbf B_0 + \tilde{\mathbf B}\).

Continuity:

\[\partial_t \tilde{\rho} + \nabla \cdot \left( (\rho_0 + \tilde{\rho}) \mathbf{u} \right) = 0\]

Momentum:

\[\partial_t (\rho \mathbf{u}) + \nabla \cdot (\rho\, \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u}) + \nabla \left( 2 q_0 \tilde{q} + \tilde{q}^2 \right) = (\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}) \times \mathbf{B}_0 + (\nabla \times \mathbf{B}_0) \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + (\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}) \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} + \nabla \cdot \left( (\mu + \mu_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \mathbf{u} \right)\]

Square root of the pressure:

\[\partial_t \tilde{q} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla (q_0 + \tilde{q}) + \frac{\gamma}{2} (q_0 + \tilde{q}) \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0\]

Induction:

\[\partial_t \tilde{\mathbf{B}} - \nabla \times \left( \mathbf{u} \times (\mathbf{B}_0 + \tilde{\mathbf{B}}) \right) + \nabla \times \left( (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \right) = 0\]

Here \(\mu_a(\mathbf{x})\) and \(\eta_a(\mathbf{x})\) are artificial viscosity and resistivity coefficients. Since \(\nabla(2 q_0 \tilde q + \tilde q^2) = \nabla \tilde p\) with \(\tilde p = p - p_0\), the ideal part (\(\mu = \eta = 0\)) is the perturbative ideal MHD system written in \(q\) instead of \(p\).

Linear model (linear=True): the terms that are nonlinear in the perturbations are dropped, i.e. \(\rho \to \rho_0\) in the continuity and momentum equations (no \(\mathbf u \cdot \nabla \mathbf u\)), the pressure force is \(\nabla (2 q_0 \tilde q) = 2 q_0 \nabla \tilde q + 2 \tilde q \nabla q_0\), the Lorentz force has no \((\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf B}) \times \tilde{\mathbf B}\), and \(\tilde q\), \(\tilde{\mathbf B}\) are advected with \(q_0\) and \(\mathbf B_0\) only:

\[\partial_t \tilde{q} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla q_0 + \frac{\gamma}{2} q_0 \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0\,, \qquad \partial_t \tilde{\mathbf{B}} - \nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{B}_0) + \nabla \times \left( (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \right) = 0\,.\]

The equations derive from the Lagrangian (with \(\rho_0\) instead of \(\rho\) in the linear model)

\[L = \int_\Omega \left( \rho \frac{|\mathbf{u}|^2}{2} - \frac{\tilde{q}^2 + 2 q_0 \hat{q}}{\gamma - 1} - \frac{|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}|^2}{2} - \mathbf{B}_0 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{B}} \right) \textrm d \mathbf{x}\,,\]

with the auxiliary variables \(\hat q\) (diagnostics.qt3) and \(\hat{\mathbf B}\) (diagnostics.bt2) advected by the perturbations only (in both models),

\[\partial_t \hat{q} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \tilde{q} + \frac{\gamma}{2} \tilde{q} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{2 q_0} \left( (\mu + \mu_a(\mathbf{x})) |\nabla \mathbf{u}|^2 + (\eta + \eta_a(\mathbf{x})) |\nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}|^2 \right)\,, \qquad \partial_t \hat{\mathbf{B}} - \nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \tilde{\mathbf{B}}) = 0\,.\]

They do not feed back on the other unknowns, but they make the energy

\[H = \int_\Omega \left( \rho \frac{|\mathbf{u}|^2}{2} + \frac{\tilde{q}^2 + 2 q_0 \hat{q}}{\gamma - 1} + \frac{|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}|^2}{2} + \mathbf{B}_0 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{B}} \right) \textrm d \mathbf{x}\]

a conserved quantity. Note that the heat produced by viscosity and resistivity goes into \(\hat q\), not into \(\tilde q\) (in the linear model, the heating is skipped with the option fast=True of the dissipative propagators).

Velocity in \(H(\textnormal{div})\) (linear=True, crank_nicolson=True, u_space="Hdiv"): the linear model can also be discretized with the velocity as a 2-form, as in LinearMHD; this is possible because the linear model has no advection of the momentum (no commutator), the only term that needs \(\mathbf u \in (H^1)^3\). classmethod doc_pde()[source]#

PDEs solved by model:

Continuity:

\[\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0\]

Momentum:

\[\partial_t (\rho \mathbf{u}) + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u}) + \rho \nabla \frac{(\rho \mathcal{U}(\rho, s))}{\partial \rho} + s \nabla \frac{(\rho \mathcal{U}(\rho, s))}{\partial s} = 0\]

Entropy:

\[\partial_t s + \nabla \cdot (s \mathbf{u}) = 0\]

where the internal energy per unit mass is \(\mathcal U(\rho) = \rho^{\gamma-1} \exp(s / \rho)\).

With divdiv_alpha \(=\alpha > 0\), the kinetic energy is \(\int \rho |\mathbf u|^2/2 + \alpha \rho (\nabla \cdot \mathbf u)^2 \,\textrm d \mathbf x\) (Hamiltonian regularization of compressions). The equations follow from the same variational principle with the momentum \(\rho \mathbf u - 2 \alpha \nabla (\rho \nabla \cdot \mathbf u)\) instead of \(\rho \mathbf u\), and the density derivative \(\alpha (\nabla \cdot \mathbf u)^2\) of the kinetic energy enters the force like \(|\mathbf u|^2/2\).
